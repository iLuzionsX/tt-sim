EVIDENCE & ASSUMPTIONS

What this model can tell you

This is a source-informed 3D explanation, not a validated crash, fire, or structural-collapse solver. The historical impact and collapse-initiation times are prescribed. The animation does not independently establish a cause. More detailed appearance does not increase the certainty of the underlying assumptions.

Documented findings

NIST attributed collapse initiation to the combined effects of aircraft damage and subsequent fires. Its proposed sequence includes core weakening, floor deformation, inward bowing, and column instability. Its tower simulations focused on collapse initiation, not a full simulation of the debris pile.

Steel was not one uniform grade

The towers used carbon and high-strength low-alloy structural steels. Perimeter plates had multiple specified yield strengths from 36 to 100 ksi (about 248–690 MPa). Core members commonly used 36 and 42 ksi steel. Floor truss components included A36 and A242 steel. Strength grade and alloy specification are different things; a single “tower steel” value would be misleading.

What is simplified here

  • Tower envelope: about 63 m square, 110 floors; roof heights about 417 m and 415 m. Floor heights are uniform in this model. Core-column locations and sections are schematic, not digitized drawings.
  • The model represents 236 perimeter columns and 47 core columns per tower. Branching lobby columns and mechanical-floor façade bands are represented visually. Connections, hat-truss load redistribution, and member-specific plate thicknesses are not resolved. Surrounding buildings are generic massing, not a surveyed Manhattan model.
  • The aircraft has an approximate 767-200 envelope and banked approach. It is clipped at the façade during penetration; damage follows a fuselage/wing/engine-shaped envelope. Holes, severed members, breakup, and fire patches are illustrative. Speed estimates and impact-floor ranges are sourced. The path is not a trajectory fit and the aircraft is not a deformable impact model.
  • Soot, broken glazing, flame patches, and smoke locate the affected region; none is a measured temperature map. Gas and steel temperatures are different; fires and heating were spatially uneven. No fluid dynamics, fuel combustion, heat-transfer field, or fireproofing-loss map is calculated.
  • The enlarged floor view exaggerates sag and bowing. The main descent is a one-dimensional post-initiation calculation: gravity accelerates an assumed upper mass, newly engulfed equal floor masses are captured with vertical momentum conservation, and a constant assumed resistance of 20% of initial upper-mass weight dissipates work. Initiation is prescribed. This model omits lateral load paths, member buckling, fracture mechanics, ejected-mass accounting, and real floor resistance; it does not predict historical collapse timing. Tilt, breakup, and core-remnant delay are illustrative. Detached fragments use gravity with approximate horizontal drag; fragment release and velocities are prescribed. Smoke and dust are procedural optical effects.
  • The steel experiment uses generic Eurocode reduction factors. It is independent of the sequence. It cannot determine a tower’s remaining load capacity or failure temperature.

Chronology and surviving cores

The other tower follows the same approximate historical clock. South Tower fires are shown only after its impact, and its main structure is gone by the North Tower’s collapse. NIST documented portions of the cores remaining briefly after the main exterior descent. The model depicts that distinction with illustrative residual sections, rather than making every part disappear at once.
